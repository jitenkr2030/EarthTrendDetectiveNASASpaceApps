## 0:00–0:25 — The research question

"EarthTrend Detective investigates how surface soil moisture has varied across Jharkhand, India, using NASA SMAP satellite data. We did not assume the state was getting drier. We tested what the data support."

## 0:25–0:55 — The data

"We analyzed NASA SMAP SPL4SMGP Version 008 surface soil moisture from April 2015 through September 2026. The study covers 984 grid cells and 138 monthly periods, totaling 135,792 cell-month records. The GPM rainfall comparison covers the common period through September 2025."

Show the study area, date range, and relevant data summary on the live dashboard.

## 0:55–1:35 — The main finding

"Our first-pass state-level trend test did not detect a statistically significant monotonic drying trend: the p-value is approximately 0.931. This does not prove drought risk is absent. It means this test and record do not support a significant state-wide monotonic decline."

Show the trend result or final-finding visualization if available.

## 1:35–2:10 — Rainfall relationship and extremes

"We found a strong same-month association between standardized soil-moisture and rainfall anomalies, with Pearson r around 0.714. The project reports that this association remains significant after its autocorrelation-aware adjustment. We also identified 5,917 extreme cell-months under our chosen thresholds: 1,656 dry and 4,261 wet. These are cell-month observations, not thousands of independent drought events."

Show the rainfall relationship and extreme-event visualizations.

## 2:10–2:40 — Why it matters

"The current evidence points to rainfall-linked variability and episodic extremes, rather than a statistically significant state-wide monotonic drying trend in our first-pass test. We report uncertainty and do not treat correlation as causation."

## 2:40–3:00 — Close

"EarthTrend Detective turns a large satellite-data record into an inspectable regional analysis. The dashboard and repository help people explore the work. Our next step is to strengthen trend sensitivity testing and the treatment of spatial dependence."
